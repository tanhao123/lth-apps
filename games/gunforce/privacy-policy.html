Gunforce – Privacy Policy

Effective 11 November 2026 · Developer: Lam Tan Hao · Windows, Android, iOS and the web

This policy covers Gunforce (“LTH - Gunforce” on the App Store), a 2D side-scrolling run-and-gun action game by the developer Lam Tan Hao, on the Microsoft Store, Google Play and the App Store, and in a web browser on the LTH Games web store. The game needs no account and does not ask for your name, age or location. This page explains every piece of information the game uses and where it goes.

Stored on your device

The game saves its progress and settings on your own device only: the stages you have reached and your last checkpoint with its score, your high score, the difficulty, the music and sound volumes, and whether the touch controls are shown. In the versions with online co-op, also a random device code for it (see Online co-op below). None of this is sent to the developer's server. It is removed when you uninstall the game, or, on the web version, when you clear this site's data in your browser.

Internet check

The game needs an internet connection. When it opens, and again before play goes on (for example before a stage), it sends a small request to a public connectivity address (Google, Cloudflare or Apple) to see whether you are online. The request carries no information about you; like any web request, it reveals your IP address to that service.

Checking for a new version

When it opens, the game asks its store (the Microsoft Store, Google Play or the App Store) whether a newer version is available. The question only identifies the game. If there is a newer version, the game asks you to update before playing on.

Feedback

The FEEDBACK page of the main menu is optional. When you press SEND, the game sends to the developer's server (run by the developer on Microsoft Azure), over an encrypted HTTPS connection:

  • your message;
  • your name and email address, only if you type them in (both optional);
  • the game's name and the kind of device (for example “Android”).

The server also records the IP address and the user agent (a short description of the app and system that sent it) with the feedback. This is used only to read your feedback and reply to you. The developer does not sell or share it, or use it for advertising. Feedback is deleted when it is no longer needed, or as soon as you ask.

Advertising on phones

On Android and iOS, the game shows ads from Google AdMob at breaks in play, never during it: a full-screen ad between stages and when a stage restarts after GAME OVER, and an optional ad you can watch to continue after GAME OVER. To serve, measure and protect these ads, Google may collect device identifiers (such as the advertising ID on Android), your IP address (from which an approximate location can be worked out), information about your device, and how you interact with the ads. See How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and Google's Privacy Policy.

  • Consent. Where the law requires it (the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), the game asks for your consent before ads load. AD PRIVACY, under OPTIONS, lets you change your choice at any time.
  • iPhone and iPad. The game does not ask for permission to track you, so Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA) is not used.
  • Your device settings let you reset your advertising ID or limit ad personalization.

Windows

The Windows version shows no ads and uses no ad network.

Web version

The web version runs in your browser from the LTH Games web store. Your progress and settings are saved in your browser's storage on your device, and clearing the site's data in your browser removes them. To keep you on the newest version, the browser also remembers which version of the game you played last.

The web store's own pages, and the game's page there, may show ads (Google AdSense) and may store small files (cookies and similar identifiers) in your browser. The web version of the game may also show ads at breaks in play, through Google's ad breaks, never during play. What these do is described in the LTH Games privacy notice, which also links to Google's explanation. The sections above on the internet check and on feedback apply to the web version as the game does them there.

Online co-op

Online co-op for 2 to 4 players is in the web version and in the Windows, Android and iOS versions that offer ONLINE CO-OP (on Android only on 64-bit phones), and players on different platforms can share a room. You use it only if you choose ONLINE CO-OP; playing alone sends nothing of this kind. There are no accounts, no names to type, no chat and no voice: players are only seats in the room (P1 to P4).

  • Players connect directly. Co-op uses a direct peer-to-peer connection between your device and the others' (WebRTC). The players in a room can therefore see each other's IP addresses. The host's game runs the room, and what you do in it (movement, shots, the state of the stage) goes straight between the players, encrypted, and not through the developer's server. Only play with people you trust to know your address.
  • The room list. While a room has a free seat, it appears in FIND A ROOM with only how many players it has, its stage and whether a game is on: never its code, a name or an address. Asking to join a room from the list sends your connection details to the host's game, and the host's game sends nothing back (its address included) unless the host lets you in. A room joined with its code asks nobody.
  • Finding each other. To find your public address, your device contacts public STUN servers run by Cloudflare and Google. They see your IP address, as any web request shows it.
  • A relay when needed. If a direct connection cannot be made, the game data can go through a relay (TURN) run by Cloudflare. The data is encrypted end to end between the players (DTLS), so the relay carries it without reading it, but Cloudflare sees the IP addresses and how much data passes. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.
  • The developer's server only introduces players. To set up a room, the game calls the developer's server (the same one as the feedback form, on Microsoft Azure), which passes each player's connection details to the host and the host's reply back. It keeps rooms in memory only, with no database. For a room it holds a room code, a secret key for the host, the game's name, the kind of platform (“WebGL”, “Windows”, “Android” or “iOS”) and the game's version, and the connection details of players who are joining (these contain IP addresses and ports). A room is removed when its host closes it, or about a minute after the host stops checking in, and a join attempt expires after 90 seconds. Restarting the server also clears all rooms. The server does not write these details to its own logs; what it does record is below.
  • Limits on abuse. The server looks at your IP address to limit how many rooms and join requests one address can make in a minute. These counters are kept in memory.
  • Records the server's host keeps. The developer's server writes a short line when a room is created or closed, with the room code, the game's name and the platform, and no IP address. Separately, the web server's host (Microsoft Azure) keeps standard access logs, which include IP addresses and the addresses requested, for 3 days and then deletes them.
  • Your device code. The game makes a random code, which is not linked to you, and saves it on your device (in your browser's storage on the web). When you join a room it is sent to the host over the direct connection, so that if you drop out you can get your seat back. It is never sent to the developer's server, and the host's game keeps it only while the room lasts. Uninstalling the game, or clearing the site's data in your browser, deletes it.
  • On iPhone and iPad, a direct connection to a player on the same Wi-Fi can make iOS ask whether the game may find devices on your local network. If you refuse, a connection to a player on your own network may fail; the game uses it only to connect to the players of a room.
  • Invite links contain the room code. Anyone who has the link can try to join the room while it is open, so share it only with the players you want.

Game engine diagnostics

The game is built with Unity. Unity may send anonymous technical details of your device, such as its model, operating system and graphics chip, to Unity Technologies to improve the engine. They do not identify you. See Unity's privacy policy for players. The game uses no other analytics or tracking tools.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them. If a child has sent feedback with a name or email address, please tell us and we will delete it at once.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted on this page with a new effective date.
