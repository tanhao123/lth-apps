GunGhost – Privacy Policy

Effective 7 November 2026 · Developer: Lam Tan Hao · Windows, Android and iOS

This policy covers GunGhost (“Gunghost” on Google Play and the App Store, “Ghost Hunter” in the game), a first-person ghost-hunting action game by the developer Lam Tan Hao, on the Microsoft Store, Google Play and the App Store. The game needs no account and does not ask for your name, age or location. This page explains every piece of information the game uses and where it goes.

Stored on your device

The game saves its settings and records on your own device only: the difficulty you chose, your best score and grade for each difficulty, and which tips you have already seen. This is never sent anywhere, and it is removed when you uninstall the game.

Internet check

The game needs an internet connection. When it opens, and again before play goes on, it sends a small request to a public connectivity address (Google, Cloudflare or Apple) to see whether you are online. The request carries no information about you; like any web request, it reveals your IP address to that service.

Checking for a new version

When it opens, the game asks its store (the Microsoft Store, Google Play or the App Store) whether a newer version is available. The question only identifies the game. If there is a newer version, the game asks you to update before playing on.

Feedback

The FEEDBACK page of the main menu is optional. When you press SEND, the game sends to the developer's server (run by the developer on Microsoft Azure), over an encrypted HTTPS connection:

  • your message;
  • your name and email address, only if you type them in (both optional);
  • the game's name and the kind of device (for example “Android”).

The server also records the IP address and the user agent (a short description of the app and system that sent it) with the feedback. This is used only to read your feedback and reply to you. The developer does not sell or share it, or use it for advertising. Feedback is deleted when it is no longer needed, or as soon as you ask.

Advertising on phones

On Android and iOS, the game shows ads from Google AdMob at breaks in play, never during it: a full-screen ad after you are defeated, and an optional ad you can watch to continue. To serve, measure and protect these ads, Google may collect device identifiers (such as the advertising ID on Android), your IP address (from which an approximate location can be worked out), information about your device, and how you interact with the ads. See How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and Google's Privacy Policy.

  • Consent. Where the law requires it (the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), the game asks for your consent before ads load. AD PRIVACY, on the main menu, lets you change your choice at any time.
  • iPhone and iPad. The game does not ask for permission to track you, so Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA) is not used.
  • Your device settings let you reset your advertising ID or limit ad personalization.

Windows

The Windows version uses no ad network. After you are defeated it may show a card marked AD, promoting another game by the same developer. The card is part of the game: it loads nothing from advertising networks and collects nothing. Its button opens that game's Microsoft Store page.

Game engine diagnostics

The game is built with Unity. Unity may send anonymous technical details of your device, such as its model, operating system and graphics chip, to Unity Technologies to improve the engine. They do not identify you. See Unity's privacy policy for players. The game uses no other analytics or tracking tools.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them. If a child has sent feedback with a name or email address, please tell us and we will delete it at once.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted on this page with a new effective date.
